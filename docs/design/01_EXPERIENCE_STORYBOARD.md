# 01 · Experience Storyboard

**The blueprint's spine.** Every chapter of the homepage, designed before implementation. Companion documents detail the systems this storyboard assumes: emotion (02), rhythm (03), motion (04), photography (05), type (06), conversion (07), purposes-at-a-glance (08), brand language (09), build order (10).

**The core question, answered first.**
*What experience should a visitor have from the first second until they contact Via-We?*

The visitor is a business owner or founder in Andhra Pradesh, most likely arriving on a phone from a WhatsApp forward or an Instagram profile. They have seen a hundred agency pages that shout. Their private fear is not "is this agency creative?" — it is *"will these people take my thing seriously, or will I be handed around and dropped?"*

So the experience is **walking into a calm room where someone finally sees the whole picture.** No noise on arrival. One drawn line that behaves like the company: it travels, it connects, it never drops. The page walks their dream down a road — setup, identity, technology, reach, people, growth — and the road ends not at a form but at a conversation that has already been started for them. They should leave with one memory: *these people won't drop me.* Everything below exists to produce that memory.

The page is **one journey in six chapters**, not six sections:

> **Arrive → The System → The Belief → The Road → The Rhythm → Begin** (+ Chrome, + Sign-off)

---

## Chapter 0 · Chrome (header, pill, menu)

- **Business goal:** Never lose a visitor who is ready; never distract one who isn't.
- **User emotion:** Orientation without awareness — chrome should be felt as manners, not seen as UI.
- **Visual goal:** At rest: mark + wordmark in ink over light. On scroll: everything hands over to a single floating pill — the page's one persistent object, calm and legible on every surface.
- **Motion goal:** The hand-off is the only chrome motion: bar fades, pill surfaces. Menu opens as one movement (panel and contents ride together). Micro-tier timing throughout.
- **Conversion goal:** After the visitor has seen The Road, the pill quietly gains a conversation affordance — the "I'm ready" exit is then never more than one tap away, at the exact moment readiness becomes possible.
- **Content goal:** Three destinations only (What we do · How we work · Contact). The menu repeats them large, adds socials and the three real contact routes. Nothing else.
- **Why it exists:** The journey needs a constant. The pill is the line's promise in miniature: always there, never in the way.
- **What disappears:** Nothing structural — Phase 2 chrome is correct. The menu's stock photo goes when the photography system lands (slot: `culture`).
- **What is added:** The post-Road conversation affordance in the pill; current-chapter awareness (the pill subtly acknowledges where you are — see 04).
- **What users remember:** Nothing. That is success. Chrome that is remembered has failed.

## Chapter 1 · Arrive (hero — two movements)

### Movement I — the overture

- **Business goal:** In five seconds: this is a serious company, it does one big thing ("we build, brand & grow businesses"), and it is unlike every agency site the visitor has seen — by being *quieter*, not louder.
- **User emotion:** **Stillness, then recognition.** The relief of no noise; then "they're talking about my dream," triggered by the eyebrow ("A business growth & execution company") and the promise stated as three calm beats.
- **Visual goal:** Cool paper field. Navy display type at three beats — "We build," / "brand & grow" / "businesses." The signature owns the right half: two curves — one solid deep navy, one cyan carrying the mark's mesh as it approaches — crossing at a node that is a *focal event*, not a 10px dot. The composition holds a charged asymmetry: type mass left, connection event right, both aimed at each other.
- **Motion goal:** The page's single orchestrated moment (Overture tier, see 04): curves draw from opposite edges → mesh resolves into the cyan curve → the node blooms at the crossing → the headline rises through its masks. One composed sequence, ~2.5s total, never repeated. Load *is* the overture — no loader exists.
- **Conversion goal:** None. Asking here is cheap. The chapter's conversion job is to earn the next scroll.
- **Content goal:** Eleven words of headline + seven of eyebrow. Nothing else competes.
- **Why it exists:** First impressions are the brand. The overture states the entire thesis wordlessly: two things travel separately, meet, and become one system.
- **What disappears:** The empty, uncharged right half. The four-line headline stack with its ragged middle.
- **What is added:** Mesh texture entering the composition; the node as event; the three-beat break.
- **What users remember:** Big calm blue type, and a line meeting another line. That image *is* the logo's meaning — planted before they ever study the logo.

### Movement II — the invitation

- **Business goal:** Convert recognition into orientation: name the five arms in one breath and offer the two exits (talk now / see the system).
- **User emotion:** "They do *all* of it." The first inkling that the vendor-juggling could end.
- **Visual goal:** Asymmetric editorial grid: the support line (the one verified ecosystem sentence) left, one warm human frame right (slot: `team` or `office` — the night-megacity plate is gone). The image is the first evidence the company is made of people.
- **Motion goal:** Editorial tier only — the support line is one of exactly two places the word-scrub survives (see 04); buttons and image rise once. Calm after the overture: dynamics, not constant intensity.
- **Conversion goal:** Primary "Start a conversation" for the already-convinced (referral traffic often is); quiet "See what we do" as the honest default path.
- **Content goal:** One sentence of scope, two actions. No paragraph of positioning — the page *is* the positioning.
- **Why it exists:** The overture is emotion; this movement is orientation. Separating them keeps both pure.
- **What disappears:** The night-city reel (tonal spoiler of the dark chapter; anonymous megacity under a Vijayawada promise).
- **What is added:** The human frame; hover-video mechanic retained for when real footage exists (slot: `culture` loop).
- **What users remember:** "One team does the whole thing" — and a first human face associated with it.

## Chapter 2 · The System (ecosystem)

- **Business goal:** Make the five real pillars legible as **one road** — the structural argument for hiring Via-We over five vendors. Route each visitor to the arm they came for while showing them the whole.
- **User emotion:** **Orientation.** "I can see the entire road — and I can see where I am on it." For the franchise investor, arriving at stage 5 and understanding stages 1–4 built it.
- **Visual goal:** The spine (the line, vertical) carries five stations. Each station: index + segment name (sea), pillar name in display, one promise, the honest scope list. **The meter breaks once:** Brand & Market — the flagship — inverts its layout and carries the section's only photograph (slot: `brand-session`). Paper deepens station by station toward the coming dark. Bridges between stations are written in the narrative voice, visibly (sentence case, sea, sized to be read — no longer disguised as chrome).
- **Motion goal:** The spine draws with progress; each station's node **joins** (blooms) as its station arrives — five small repetitions of the hero's crossing event. On mobile the spine survives as a slim in-column rail: the story must not be desktop-only.
- **Conversion goal:** At the terminus — after Franchise — the road pauses: one quiet CTA ("Start the conversation") for visitors whose question is already answered. The terminus line hands the rest onward: *"Here's what that road feels like —"*
- **Content goal:** Five names, five promises, thirty scope truths, four bridges, one terminus. Every scope item from the truth document; not one invented capability.
- **Why it exists:** This is the argument. Everything before it is invitation; everything after it is proof and feeling.
- **What disappears:** The four-viewport flat spot (five identical stanzas); bridges at 2.57:1 contrast; the dead band under each scope list; the desktop-only thread.
- **What is added:** The pattern-break media station; deepening ground; mobile spine; terminus + quiet CTA.
- **What users remember:** A vertical line with five stops. The image of *sequence* — that each service feeds the next — which is the single idea competitors can't claim.

---

## Chapter 3 · The Belief (ex-Partner)

- **Business goal:** Give the company a *soul* on the page. The visitor now knows what Via-We does; this chapter says why it works this way — the founder's conviction, without one unverifiable word.
- **User emotion:** **Alignment.** "They've named exactly what I've been through." The vendor-gap pain named plainly, then the refusal: you explain it once.
- **Visual goal:** Mid-tone ground (paper-deep) — the first tonal step toward the dark chapter. The chapter's typography is its image: a short manifesto set large. Two photographs only: one human (slot: `business-discussion`), one process (slot: `whiteboard`). Gains its wayfinding eyebrow like every other chapter.
- **Motion goal:** Almost still. The manifesto is the second and final home of the word-scrub — conviction delivered at reading pace. Stillness after the systematic chapter is the dynamic.
- **Conversion goal:** None explicit. Belief chapters that sell undermine themselves.
- **Content goal:** Rewritten from scratch: the dream taken personally; the one-roof refusal; execution over advice. First person plural. Every sentence passable as something a person who has built businesses would say aloud. **The duplicate "not five vendors" argument moves fully to Chapter 2 and dies here.**
- **Why it exists:** The Phase 2 page argued the same point twice and never said *who Via-We is*. This chapter is the missing "who."
- **What disappears:** "Fewer handoffs" as a repeated structural argument; the anonymous coworking photos.
- **What is added:** The manifesto voice; the eyebrow; purposeful imagery.
- **What users remember:** One sentence of conviction — the chapter should be written so that its opening line is quotable in a WhatsApp forward.

---

## Chapter 4 · The Road (pathway — the dark chapter)

- **Business goal:** Let the visitor *live* the journey with their own business in mind — the emotional peak that converts understanding into wanting.
- **User emotion:** **Immersion → projection.** Lights down, one story: "that's my shop being set up; that's my brand being decided; those are my first hires." The chapter works when the visitor stops evaluating Via-We and starts imagining themselves.
- **Visual goal:** Near-black navy; the mesh lives here as a barely-there field (the network holding the dark together). Six stages — Set up, Brand, Build, Reach, Hire, Expand — each one display word, one operational sentence, one photograph (slots: `project-planning`, `brand-session`, `development`, `campaigns`, `meetings`, `outdoor`). Entry is a **threshold**: the preceding ground deepens and the line dives; exit hands cleanly to the light rhythm chapter.
- **Motion goal:** The pin remains; inside it, stages **travel instead of cut** — plates crossfade with directional consistency (the road always moves forward), names slide along the scrub direction, progress bars (already correct) extend their logic to the content. The visitor's scroll is the road; steps in it are potholes.
- **Conversion goal:** Structural: this is the peak — immediately after it, the pill's conversation affordance appears (Chapter 0). The chapter itself stays uninterrupted; a CTA inside the cinema breaks the spell.
- **Content goal:** Six stages, six sentences, each operationally concrete (dates, groups, reports — not adjectives).
- **Why it exists:** Every reference-grade site has one immersive set-piece. This is Via-We's — and it is the journey ladder (dream → setup → identity → technology → marketing → people → growth → expansion) rendered as cinema.
- **What disappears:** Hard-cut stage swaps; the unmarked cliff at entry; Western stock plates.
- **What is added:** Threshold entry/exit; continuous choreography; mesh field; directional grammar.
- **What users remember:** The feeling of moving down a dark road where each stop was handled — the page's emotional signature.

---

## Chapter 5 · The Rhythm (approach)

- **Business goal:** De-risk the decision. After wanting comes the practical fear: *what is it actually like to work with them?* Answer with the real operating rhythm.
- **User emotion:** **Reassurance.** "No mystery — I can see the week-one reality." This is also the page's honest proof layer: we cannot show client results yet, but our own process is ours to show.
- **Visual goal:** Return to light — relief after the dark chapter, and the contrast makes both stronger. Wide band photograph (slot: `strategy` or `business-workshop`), then four steps in a two-column ledger. Proof-of-method concreteness: the group, the plan before production, the readable report.
- **Motion goal:** Quietest chapter on the page. Steps arrive whole (no scrub); the line appears only as the held rules between steps — after the road's drama, calm *is* the message.
- **Conversion goal:** Pre-decision settling. The chapter lowers the perceived cost of saying hello; the closing chapter collects.
- **Content goal:** Four steps in operational language, each one traceable to the real workflow in the truth document. No values-poster words.
- **Why it exists:** Trust, on a page forbidden (correctly) from testimonials and logos, must come from *specificity*. This is where specificity lives.
- **What disappears:** Nothing structural — Phase 2's shape was right. Generic step-copy tightens further.
- **What is added:** Proof-of-method texture (named artifacts of week one); the band photo from the real system.
- **What users remember:** "One conversation, one group, a plan before production, a report I can read." Four facts that survive retelling.

---

## Chapter 6 · Begin (closing)

- **Business goal:** Collect the decision. One dominant action aligned with how Via-We actually sells: WhatsApp.
- **User emotion:** **Resolve.** The visitor arrives here carrying the whole journey; the chapter must feel like the road's destination, not a marketing section.
- **Visual goal:** Dark again — the page closes where the road ran. The tagline has its single styled appearance here, above the invitation: after five chapters of demonstration, *Your Dreams, Our Aim* lands as a summary instead of a slogan. The line resolves: the journey's thread arrives and **joins** at a final node beside the action.
- **Motion goal:** One held moment. Tagline and headline rise once; the node blooms once; nothing loops or pulses. The most important click on the page deserves the least distraction.
- **Conversion goal:** **Binary, not ternary.** WhatsApp dominant, with a pre-filled opener ("Hi Via-We — I'd like to talk about my business."), opening in its own context. Call secondary for the phone-first generation. Email leaves the row (it lives in the footer). One decision, two doors.
- **Content goal:** Tagline · one headline ("Tell us where you want to go next." survives — it is good) · one promise sentence · two actions.
- **Why it exists:** Every journey needs an arrival. This is the conversation the whole page exists to start.
- **What disappears:** The three-way CTA row; the same-tab WhatsApp exit; the stock corridor backdrop (slot: `office` at dusk, or held navy until real photography).
- **What is added:** Tagline moment; pre-filled opener; the line's resolution node.
- **What users remember:** That contacting Via-We felt like the natural end of a road, not a form at the bottom of a website.

---

## Chapter 7 · Sign-off (footer)

- **Business goal:** Permanence and legitimacy — the legal, physical, findable company behind the promise.
- **User emotion:** Confirmation. "Real address, real people, real company."
- **Visual goal:** The page's one **generous logo presentation**: the mark at scale beside the wordmark — the place the logo's mesh and curves finally register in full. Then the three utility columns (verbatim address, human contact routes, socials) and the legal line.
- **Motion goal:** None beyond link manners. The sign-off is still.
- **Conversion goal:** Utility capture for methodical visitors (the email lives here); nothing competes with Chapter 6.
- **Content goal:** Verified facts only, spellings verbatim per intake rules.
- **Why it exists:** Trust's last mile is boring and factual — and the brand deserves a signature.
- **What disappears:** The brand-silent business card; sub-AA stone-on-ink labels.
- **What is added:** The brand block; corrected contrast tokens.
- **What users remember:** The mark, at a size where they finally *see* it — the last image before they leave is the logo meaning they now understand.

---

## Storyboard invariants

1. Facts only from the truth document and intake; unverified items never render.
2. The line **never drops**: every chapter hands to the next; the only broken line permitted anywhere is an error state — and it visibly repairs (see 04).
3. Each chapter owns exactly one message; anything serving another chapter's message moves there.
4. Mobile carries the full narrative — no desktop-only story devices.
5. Reduced motion receives the complete story, still.
